# DTM 2004: research dossier

The fifth season of the revived DTM had 10 championship races:
- 6 in Germany (Hockenheim twice)
- Estoril (POR), Adria (ITA), Zandvoort (NED) and Brno (CZE)
- plus a non-championship invitation race in Shanghai (CHN, 18 Jul).

The season ran from 18 Apr to 3 Oct.
Mattias Ekström (Audi Sport Team Abt, A4 DTM) won the title with 74 points, ahead of Gary Paffett (57) and Christijan Albers (50). Audi won the drivers', teams' and manufacturers' titles in its first works season since 1992.

## Regulations (changes vs 2003)
- **Saloons:**
  - The new cars were based on **4-door saloons**: Audi A4, Mercedes C-Class and Opel Vectra GTS.
  - Smaller independent teams could run **year-old 2003 coupés** for one more season to save costs [en.wiki].
  - de.wiki presents the move to saloons as an ITR attempt to attract new manufacturers.
- **Race format:** unchanged from 2003. One race of about 1 hour, about 150–170 km, with **2 mandatory pit stops** (tyres; refuelling allowed and needed) [en.wiki].
  - The A4 DTM had a 70 L tank, and de.wiki states that refuelling was mandatory at the time.
- **Qualifying:** timed session plus top-10 Superpole, as in 2003 (no 2004 change documented).
- **Points:**
  - Drivers: **10-8-6-5-4-3-2-1** to the top 8.
  - Manufacturers: all cars score.
  - Teams: 2-car entries.
- **Tyres:** Dunlop SP Sport Maxx with new, larger sizes: **front 265/660-R18, rear 280/660-R18**, for more mechanical grip [en.wiki; de.wiki A4 DTM].
- **Fuel:** Shell became the control fuel partner (2004 to mid-2005) [en.wiki].
- **Minimum weight:** **1080 kg including driver** for the 2004 cars (A4, Vectra and C-Class infobox data). It dropped to 1050 kg in 2005.

## Teams and people
- **Audi:**
  - Works return in partnership with Abt: Audi Sport Team Abt (#5 Ekström, #6 Tomczyk) and Audi Sport Team Abt Sportsline (#11 Abt, #12 Kristensen).
  - **Team Joest** (Audi Sport Infineon Team Joest) returned to the DTM for the first time since 1996, with Pirro and Biela.
  - Shanghai one-offs: Capello (Joest A4) and Terting (Abt TT-R 2003).
- **Mercedes:**
  - HWA ran Vodafone #1 Schneider, DaimlerChrysler Bank #2 Albers, and C-Klasse AMG-Mercedes #7 Paffett and #8 Alesi.
  - Persson (Original-Teile): M. Winkelhock and Mücke.
  - Rosberg (Sonax Dark Dog): Janiš and Mayländer.
- **Opel:**
  - Phoenix: Fässler, Aïello and Dumbreck.
  - Holzer: Frentzen, Reuter and Scheider.
  - Euroteam: Bleekemolen.
  - en.wiki's team table registers the Opels as 2-car entries "OPC Team 2/3/4" mixing cars from Phoenix and Holzer; de.wiki groups them by operating team.
- **Team principals:** not documented in the sources checked. General knowledge (unverified): Reinhold Joest, Hans-Jürgen Abt.

## Storylines
- **Ekström's title.** Ekström won 4 championship races (Adria, Lausitz, Zandvoort and Brno). He was champion in Audi's comeback year.
- **Paffett's breakthrough.** Paffett won 3 championship races (Hockenheim 1, Norisring and Nürburgring) plus the non-championship Shanghai race, but was disqualified at Lausitz.
- **Opel struggled with the Vectra.** Its best result was Reuter's 3rd at Oschersleben. GM announced Opel's exit at the end of 2004, effective after 2005 [de.wiki].
- **Zandvoort:** Dumbreck's Vectra rolled several times and he was unhurt. The race was stopped and restarted for the final 10 laps after a tyre change the drivers had demanded because of debris.
- **Shanghai street race:** abandoned after Mayländer hit a manhole cover lifted by other cars.
- **Calendar changes:** Estoril and Brno were new. The A1-Ring was dropped (it was being rebuilt), as was Donington.
- **Driver line-up:** newcomers Frentzen, Kristensen, Biela, Pirro, M. Winkelhock and Janiš. Joachim Winkelhock retired, and Alain Menu left the series.

## Unverified
- **Zandvoort total race laps and distance:** de.wiki gives only the 10-lap restart part (43.070 km).
- **Weight concessions for year-old coupés:** not documented. (For 2005, touringcartimes and motorsport.com mention 2004 cars carried over at 1035 kg and a placement-based handicap weight. Those are 2005 rules.)
- **Audi engine origin:** en.wiki claims a rebadged Mugen MF308-based unit built by Neil Brown. That claim is poorly sourced and unverified.
- **Opel team split:** the official 2-car pairings (en.wiki OPC Team 2/3/4) vs de.wiki's per-operator totals. Both are given in standings_other.csv.
- **Speedsport's Abt split** labels #11/#12 as 113 points, which contradicts the arithmetic: Ekström 74 + Tomczyk 39 = 113, so 113 belongs to #5/#6.
- **Tyre allocation, Superpole details for 2004, and any success ballast:** not sourced.
- **Team bases:** see the 2000 note. Joest's base (Wald-Michelbach) is from general knowledge.
