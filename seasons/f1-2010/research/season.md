# 2010 Formula One World Championship: research dossier

## Overview
- 61st F1 World Championship. 19 rounds from 14 March (Bahrain) to 14 November (Abu Dhabi).
- New race: the Korean Grand Prix (Yeongam). The Canadian Grand Prix returned.
- 12 teams, 24 cars, 27 race drivers. Three new teams: Lotus Racing, Hispania Racing (HRT) and Virgin Racing. Sauber raced on under the "BMW Sauber F1 Team" entrant name after BMW left. Brawn GP became Mercedes GP, and Michael Schumacher returned.
- Champion: **Sebastian Vettel** (Red Bull Racing, Red Bull RB6), 256 pts, ahead of Alonso (252), Webber (242) and Hamilton (240). Four drivers could still win the title at the last round. Vettel led the championship for the first time after the final race. After round 6 he was level on points with Webber.
- Constructors: Red Bull Racing 498 (first title), McLaren 454, Ferrari 396, Mercedes 214, Renault 163.
- Five winners: Vettel 5, Alonso 5, Webber 4, Hamilton 3, Button 2.
- Last season of Bridgestone as the single tyre supplier.

## Regulations
### Points
- New for 2010: 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 for the top 10 (was 10-8-6-5-4-3-2-1 for the top 8). Classified at 90% of the winner's laps.

### Qualifying
- Q1 (20 min) and Q2 (15 min) each eliminate 7 cars, top 10 in Q3. Q3 ran on low fuel because of the refuelling ban. The top 10 had to start the race on the tyres used in Q3.
- No 107% rule in 2010 (it came back in 2011).

### Tyres (Bridgestone)
- Dry compounds: hard, medium, soft, supersoft. The softer compound of the pair was marked with a green stripe (en.wiki race articles).
- Two dry compounds per race (prime and option). Both had to be used in a dry race.
- 11 sets per driver per weekend (down from 14 in 2009); 3 sets were for practice only. Front tyres narrowed from 270 mm to 245 mm. Wheel covers and rim heaters banned.
- After Canada, Bridgestone said it would choose compounds further apart from the German Grand Prix on (en.wiki season article).

### No DRS, no KERS
- DRS did not exist yet (from 2011).
- KERS was allowed but the teams (FOTA) agreed not to use it in 2010 (en.wiki).
- McLaren's driver-operated "F-duct" rear-wing stall was used by several teams in 2010 and banned for 2011. Double diffusers were legal (banned for 2011).

### Other rules
- Refuelling during the race banned from 2010. Cars got 20-22 cm longer for the bigger fuel tank (en.wiki).
- Minimum weight 620 kg with driver (was 605 kg).
- Survival cell, roll structures, impact structures and wheels homologated for the season.
- 2.4 V8, 18,000 rpm, engines frozen, 8 engines per driver per season. Gearbox for 4 events (general knowledge, not checked).
- A fixed pool of stewards including former drivers.
- In-season testing banned.

## Story of the season
- **Red Bull fastest, not always reliable.** The RB6 started from grid 1 in 15 of 19 races (Vettel 10, Webber 5). Vettel lost likely wins to failures, for example a spark-plug problem in Bahrain (finished 4th; cause from general knowledge) and an engine failure while leading in Korea. Red Bull scored 0 points in Korea.
- **Four-way fight.** Leader after each round: Alonso (rounds 1-2), Massa (3), Button (4-5), Webber (6-7), Hamilton (8-11), Webber (12), Hamilton (13), Webber (14-16), Alonso (17-18), Vettel (19). Four drivers could still win the title before Abu Dhabi.
- **McLaren** won 4 of the first 8 races (Button 2, Hamilton 2), then scored less after the summer: 278 points in rounds 1-10, 176 in rounds 11-19.
- **Ferrari** won the opener (1-2 in Bahrain), then lost pace in the middle of the season (4 points in Valencia, 0 at Silverstone), and came back with wins in Germany, Monza, Singapore and Korea. 165 points in rounds 1-10, 231 in rounds 11-19.
- **Finale in Abu Dhabi.** Vettel won from pole. Alonso finished 7th after a pit-stop strategy that left him behind Petrov's Renault (general knowledge). Vettel won the title by 4 points.
- **Renault**: Kubica 2nd in Australia, 3rd in Monaco and Belgium. **Williams**: Hülkenberg took pole in Brazil.
- **New teams** scored no points. Lotus Racing was 10th on Kovalainen's 12th place in Japan.
