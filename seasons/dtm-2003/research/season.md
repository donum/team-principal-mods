# DTM 2003: research dossier

The fourth season of the revived DTM had 10 events with **one race each**: 6 in Germany plus Adria (ITA), Donington (GBR), the A1-Ring (AUT) and Zandvoort (NED). It ran from 27 Apr to 5 Oct, opening and closing at Hockenheim.
Bernd Schneider (Vodafone AMG-Mercedes) won his 4th title with 68 points, ahead of team-mate Christijan Albers (64) and Marcel Fässler (57). Mercedes won 9 of the 10 races.

## Regulations (changes vs 2002)
- **Race format:**
  - The qualifying race was dropped. One points race of about **60 minutes** replaced the earlier 40-minute races.
  - Distances rose to as much as **~170 km**; the Norisring went from 44 to 72 laps [de.wiki; AutoBild 27 Feb and 9 Apr 2003].
  - All races started at 14:00, live on ARD or ZDF in turn [AutoBild].
- **Pit stops:**
  - **2 mandatory stops** with tyre changes.
  - **Refuelling** came back. AutoBild says at least one stop had to include fuel; this was a practical need rather than a written rule.
  - Maximum tank size 70 L. Fuel went in through a funnel-type churn, NASCAR-style.
  - Audi's Junior team used 8 mechanics for tyres and 2 for fuel [AutoBild; Audi press].
- **Tyres:**
  - Tyre warmers banned.
  - A mandatory pit stop also applied on Saturday [AutoBild via search; details unverified].
  - Dunlop was the single supplier.
- **Qualifying:**
  1. A 20-minute timed session with a maximum of 9 laps per driver. It set grid positions 11 and below and the running order for Superpole.
  2. After a break of 5 minutes (crash.net, motorsport.com) or 10 minutes (AutoBild), the **Superpole**: the top 10 each run one flying lap in reverse order, which sets grid positions 1–10.
- **Points:**
  - **10-8-6-5-4-3-2-1** to the top 8.
  - A new **manufacturers' championship** in which all cars score [motorsport.com Dec 2002; AutoBild].
  - AutoBild says the teams' championship was replaced. de.wiki and en.wiki still list team standings.
- **Engines:** each two-car team got **3 engines for the season** (previously 4), used alternately. About 500 hp per AutoBild; ~460–470 hp per other sources.
- **Unchanged:** 4.0 V8, 2x28 mm restrictors, rear-wheel drive, 1080 kg (TT-R data), year-old cars allowed.
  - motorsport.com's December 2002 plan was 4 new cars each for Abt, Mercedes and Opel. Abt and Opel would add 2 year-old cars each, and Mercedes 4 year-old cars.

## Teams and people
- **Mercedes, HWA works cars:**
  - #3 Schneider (Vodafone) and #4 Albers (Express-Service).
  - #9 Fässler and #10 Alesi (AMG-Mercedes).
- **Mercedes customer teams:**
  - Persson expanded to 3 cars: Jäger, Mayländer and Kaneishi (ARTA livery).
  - Rosberg ran Huisman, replaced by Gary Paffett from rd 3, plus Mücke.
  - Manthey withdrew.
- **Audi:**
  - Abt Sportsline ran Hasseröder (#1 Aïello, #2 Abt) and PlayStation 2 Red Bull (#5 Ekström, #6 Wendlinger).
  - The S line Audi Junior Team (#14 Tomczyk, #15 Terting) was supported by First Choice Racing.
  - Audi supported the Junior team officially [Audi press].
- **Opel:** Holzer (Reuter, Menu), Phoenix (Scheider, Dumbreck) and Euroteam (J. Winkelhock, Bleekemolen).
- **Team principals:** not documented in the sources checked.

## Storylines
- **Alzen's exit.** Alzen quit HWA days before rd 1. Albers was promoted from Rosberg and went on to win 4 races, finishing 4 points short of Schneider.
- **Mercedes dominance:** 9 wins in 10 races. Audi's only win was Aïello at Nürburgring 2.
- **Opel:** best results were Scheider's pole at Zandvoort and Dumbreck's 2nd at Lausitz.
- **Nürburgring 2:** qualifying was held at night.
- **Calendar changes:** Adria was a new venue. Zolder and the Sachsenring were dropped, and the Nürburgring hosted two rounds.
