# 2012 Formula One World Championship: research dossier

## Overview
- 63rd F1 World Championship. 20 rounds from 18 March (Melbourne) to 25 November (Interlagos). The first calendar with 20 races (the old record was 19, en.wiki).
- New and returning races: the United States Grand Prix at the new Circuit of the Americas (Austin), and Bahrain, which was cancelled in 2011. Turkey was dropped.
- 12 teams, 24 cars, 25 race drivers. The only mid-season driver change: Jérôme d'Ambrosio replaced the banned Romain Grosjean at Monza.
- Renamed teams: Renault became **Lotus F1 Team** (Enstone), Team Lotus became **Caterham F1 Team**, Virgin became **Marussia F1 Team**, Hispania became **HRT Formula 1 Team**.
- Kimi Räikkönen returned after two years in rallying. Michael Schumacher retired at the end of the season.
- Champion: **Sebastian Vettel** (Red Bull Racing, Red Bull RB8), 281 pts, three points ahead of Fernando Alonso (278). Third title in a row. Decided at the final round.
- Constructors: Red Bull Racing 460, Ferrari 400, McLaren 378, Lotus 303, Mercedes 142.
- Eight different winners. Seven different winners in the first seven races, a record for one season.

## Regulations
### Points
- 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 for the top 10. No points for pole or fastest lap. No half-points race in 2012.
- A car is classified if it completes 90% of the winner's laps.

### Qualifying
- Knockout format. Q1 20 minutes, the slowest 7 cars are out (positions 18-24). Q2 15 minutes, the next 7 are out (11-17). Q3 10 minutes, top 10 for the pole.
- 107% rule: a car slower than 107% of the fastest Q1 time may start only with the stewards' permission. HRT failed in Australia (both cars) and were not allowed to start. Karthikeyan was outside 107% in Spain but was allowed to start.
- The top 10 must start the race on the tyres they used for their fastest Q3 lap. Cars from 11th back have a free choice.
- DRS could be used anywhere on the track in practice and qualifying in 2012 (from 2013 only in the race zones).

### Tyres (Pirelli, single supplier)
- Dry compounds (P Zero): hard (silver band), medium (white), soft (yellow), supersoft (red). Intermediate and full wet (Cinturato) for rain.
- Pirelli brought two dry compounds to each race, the harder "prime" and the softer "option".
- In a dry race each driver must use both dry compounds. This does not apply if intermediate or wet tyres are used.
- For 2012 Pirelli made the compounds closer together. Pirelli predicted about 0.7 s per lap between the harder and softer compound, against 1.5 s in 2011 (en.wiki season article).
- Per-weekend tyre allocation (number of sets) was not in the fetched sources: unknown.

### DRS (drag reduction system)
- Driver-operated rear-wing flap, introduced in 2011.
- In the race: only in the marked zone(s), only if the car is within one second of the car ahead at the detection point. Not on the first two laps of the race, and not for two laps after a safety-car restart. Race control can disable it, for example in the wet.
- Practice and qualifying: free use anywhere in 2012.

### KERS
- Kinetic energy recovery system, optional. 60 kW, 400 kJ per lap, so about 6.67 s of extra power per lap. Which of the small teams ran it at which race is not in the fetched sources.

### Other rules
- No refuelling during the race (banned since 2010).
- Minimum weight 640 kg, car with driver.
- 2.4-litre V8 engines, rev limit 18,000 rpm, engine development frozen. 8 engines per driver for the season; a ninth engine costs a 10-place grid penalty.
- A gearbox must last 5 consecutive events. An early change costs a 5-place grid penalty. The 2011 "joker" free gearbox change was dropped for 2012 (en.wiki).
- In-season testing ban, with one exception: a three-day test at Mugello from 1 May 2012, before the Spanish Grand Prix. HRT did not take part. Teams had 15 test days in total, so pre-season testing was cut back.
- Technical changes for 2012 (en.wiki season article):
  - Exhaust-blown diffusers banned. Exhaust exits had to be higher up and away from the diffuser. In February the FIA rewrote the ECU software to close a loophole.
  - "Reactive ride height" systems (proposed by Lotus) banned in January 2012.
  - Maximum nose height cut from 62.5 cm to 55 cm, which led to the "stepped" or "platypus" noses. McLaren did not use this shape.
  - All cars had to pass the FIA crash tests before pre-season testing.
  - After the German Grand Prix (Red Bull engine maps questioned, Red Bull cleared) the FIA tightened the throttle-map rules from the Hungarian Grand Prix: teams had to keep to a reference map from one of the first four races.

## Story of the season
- **Open start.** Seven different drivers from five teams won the first seven races: Button (McLaren), Alonso (Ferrari), Rosberg (Mercedes), Vettel (Red Bull), Maldonado (Williams), Webber (Red Bull), Hamilton (McLaren). Rosberg's China win was the first for a works Mercedes team since 1955. Maldonado's Spain win was the first for Williams since 2004.
- **Tyres.** The 2012 Pirelli compounds were close together and teams struggled to keep them in their working temperature "window". Small changes in track temperature moved teams up and down the order from race to race. Lotus (Räikkönen, Grosjean) was often very fast in the race but had only one win. (The "window" description is the usual press summary of 2012; the fetched sources only give Pirelli's 0.7 s compound gap.)
- **Alonso leads.** Ferrari's F2012 was slow at the start: Alonso qualified 12th, 9th, 9th and 9th in the first four races. He still won in Malaysia (rain) and Valencia (from 11th on the grid). He led the championship after rounds 2 and 6, and then from round 8 to round 15. His biggest lead was 40 points, after Hungary (164 to Webber's 124).
- **Spa and Suzuka.** Grosjean caused a first-corner crash at Spa that took out Alonso and Hamilton, and was banned for Monza. Alonso also retired after a first-lap collision in Japan.
- **Red Bull's second half.** Red Bull scored more strongly from Singapore on (the fetched sources do not list its upgrades part by part). From Singapore Vettel won four races in a row (Singapore, Japan, Korea, India). He took the lead in Korea. In Abu Dhabi he started from the pit lane after being excluded from qualifying and still finished 3rd.
- **McLaren's form.** McLaren started from grid 1 eight times, as often as Red Bull, but lost points to retirements (7 DNFs). Hamilton retired from pole in Singapore (gearbox) and Abu Dhabi (fuel pressure or electrical, the sources differ). McLaren won the last two races (Hamilton in Austin, Button in Brazil).
- **Finale.** In a race with rain in Brazil, Vettel spun after a first-lap collision with Bruno Senna. He recovered to 6th. Alonso finished 2nd. Vettel won the title by 3 points and became the youngest triple champion, at 25.
- **Mercedes faded.** 105 points in rounds 1-10, 37 in rounds 11-20, and no points in rounds 15-19.
- **Zero-point teams.** Caterham, Marussia and HRT never scored. Petrov's 11th place for Caterham in Brazil took 10th in the constructors' championship from Marussia (Pic 12th in Brazil and Glock 12th in Singapore).

- Rounds 1-7: 7 different winners from 5 teams (McLaren 2, Red Bull 2, Ferrari 1, Mercedes 1, Williams 1). Grid 1: Red Bull 3, McLaren 2, Mercedes 1, Williams 1.
- Rounds 1-10: 7 different winners. Wins: Red Bull 3, Ferrari 3, McLaren 2, Mercedes 1, Williams 1. Grid 1: Red Bull 4, McLaren 2, Ferrari 2, Mercedes 1, Williams 1.
- Rounds 11-20: 4 different winners. Wins: McLaren 5, Red Bull 4, Lotus 1. Grid 1: McLaren 6, Red Bull 4.
- Season: 8 different winners. Wins: Red Bull 7, McLaren 7, Ferrari 3, Mercedes 1, Lotus 1, Williams 1. Grid 1: Red Bull 8, McLaren 8, Ferrari 2, Mercedes 1, Williams 1.
- Wet or mixed sessions: Malaysia race (heavy rain, red flag), Britain and Germany qualifying (wet, both Alonso poles), Brazil race (rain).
