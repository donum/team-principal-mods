# 2005 DTM: research dossier

## Overview
- 6th season of the "new" DTM (since 2000). 11 rounds, one race per weekend. Season ran from Hockenheim (17 Apr) to Hockenheim (23 Oct).
- Rounds: Hockenheim, Lausitz ×2, Spa, Brno, Oschersleben, Norisring, Nürburgring, Zandvoort, Istanbul, Hockenheim.
- Calendar changes:
  - Spa and Istanbul replaced Estoril and Adria.
  - Avignon and Moscow appeared on the original calendar but were cancelled (de.wiki DTM). They were replaced by Brno and a second Lausitz round.
- **Champion:** Gary Paffett (HWA AMG-Mercedes C-Klasse 2005) with 84 points and 5 wins. Mattias Ekström was 2nd with 71.
- Mercedes won 8 of the 11 races.
- **Teams' title:** DaimlerChrysler Bank AMG-Mercedes (Paffett/Alesi), 106 points.
- **Manufacturers' title:** Mercedes-Benz 205, Audi 179, Opel 45.
- Last season for Opel. The withdrawal was announced at the end of 2004 because of GM cost cuts, so Opel ran 2005 on a reduced budget.
- Field: 20 cars. Audi 8, Mercedes 8, Opel 4.

## Regulations
- **Cars:** silhouette prototypes with a CFRP monocoque, front-mounted 4.0 L naturally aspirated 90° V8, rear-wheel drive and a sequential 6-speed gearbox.
  - Control parts: gearbox (Xtrac/Hewland), AP Racing brakes, Bosch electronics, rear wing (de.wiki DTM).
  - Engines are air-restricted ("Luftmengenbegrenzer", de.wiki Audi A4 DTM), giving about 340–350 kW (≈460 hp) and more than 500 Nm.
- **Eligibility:** cars built to 2000–2003 rules were banned, so only 2004 and 2005 cars could race.
  - Unmodified 2005-spec cars were declared eligible for 2006 and 2007 (motorsport.com / touringcartimes, Mar 2005).
- **Weights:**
  - 2005 cars: 1,050 kg (Opel and Audi car articles; Auto Bild).
  - 2004 "Jahreswagen": 1,035 kg (motorsport.com, touringcartimes).
  - Auto Bild puts year-old cars 15 kg below the new cars' 1,050 kg.
- **Handicap weight (new for 2005)** — a brand placement handicap that applies only to the current-spec cars:
  - The winning brand adds 10 kg at the next race.
  - The 2nd-placed brand keeps its weight.
  - The 3rd-placed brand removes 10 kg.
  - The maximum is ±20 kg. Mercedes already carried +20 kg by Spa (Autosport, 5 May 2005).
  - The handicap applied to all cars of the successful brand and spec, not to a single driver.
- **Race format** (unchanged since 2003):
  - One race of about 160–170 km per weekend, roughly one hour.
  - **Two mandatory pit stops** with tyre changes.
  - Refuelling from fuel cans was allowed because the 70 L tank was retained (de.wiki DTM for 2002 onward; the car articles confirm refuelling in this era).
- **Qualifying:** a timed session, then a single-lap **Super Pole** for the top 10, which set grid positions 1–10 (de.wiki DTM, 2003 format). The 2005 round report shows "Q" and "SP" columns.
- **Points:** 10-8-6-5-4-3-2-1 to the top 8.
  - Teams' championship: two-car teams as entered.
  - Manufacturers' championship: also held.
- **Tyres:**
  - Dunlop control tyre (SP Sport Maxx): 265/660-R18 front, 280/660-R18 rear.
  - In 2005 each car had 9 sets per weekend (36 tyres). This is inferred from the 2006 cut of "two sets to seven".
- **Fuel:** Shell until round 6. From round 7 (Nürburgring), Aral Ultimate 100 RON.

## Teams and people
- **HWA (Affalterbach)** ran the Mercedes works effort.
- **Persson Motorsport:** team principal Ingmar Persson.
- **Mücke Motorsport:** replaced Team Rosberg in the Mercedes line-up.
- **Abt Sportsline (Kempten):** Hans-Jürgen Abt.
- **Joest (Wald-Michelbach):** team director Ralf Jüttner. Joest expanded from two to four cars.
- **Holzer (Augsburg):** Günther Holzer.
- **Opel team assignment conflict:**
  - de.wiki and en.wiki season tables put #9 Fässler and #10 Frentzen (GMAC/Stern Team OPC) at Holzer, and #11 Aïello and #12 Reuter ("Team OPC") at Phoenix.
  - The Holzer and Opel Vectra articles, and the en.wiki "Frentzen and Aïello swapped seats" note, say the reverse: Holzer = Aïello/Reuter, Phoenix = Fässler/Frentzen. `entries.csv` follows the latter.
- **Audi motorsport head:** Dr Wolfgang Ullrich.
- **MG Rover:** planned a Zytek-built MG ZT entry, which never raced because of the company's insolvency.

## Storylines
- **Mika Häkkinen**, the two-time F1 champion, debuted with HWA and won his 3rd race at Spa from pole.
- **Paffett vs Ekström:** Paffett won 5 races. Ekström kept the fight open with 4 wins (de.wiki DTM: "mit Glück und durch ein paar Fehler Paffetts").
- **Rookies:** Mercedes promoted F3 drivers Jamie Green (2004 F3 Euro champion), Bruno Spengler and Alexandros Margaritis.
- **Opel's lame-duck season:** downsized from 6 to 4 cars. Euroteam left. Dumbreck, Scheider and Bleekemolen lost their seats.
- **Jean Alesi** won the opener from 5th on the grid.
- **Team orders:** I found no documented 2005 team-order scandal in the sources read. The well-known team-order rows came in 2007 (see dtm-2007).
