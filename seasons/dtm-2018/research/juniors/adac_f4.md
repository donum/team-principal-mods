# ADAC Formula 4 2018 (ADAC F4 Germany)

Fourth season of the ADAC's FIA Formula 4 series. Official name "ADAC F4 Germany certified by FIA powered by Abarth" (de.wikipedia). Lowest feeder series in the pack. It replaced the ADAC Formel Masters in 2015.

## Car
- One-make: Tatuus F4-T014 chassis, Abarth 1.4-litre T-Jet turbo engine, Pirelli tyres (de.wikipedia; Pirelli deal extended to 2020).
- The Tatuus and Abarth combination is confirmed. The 2013 template car (Dallara Formulino with VW engine) is gone.

## Format
- 7 race weekends and 20 races. Six weekends had 3 races. The Hockenheim F1 weekend (round 5) had only 2.
- Usual pattern: races 1 and 2 on Saturday, race 3 on Sunday. Race 3 has no pole position listed, so its grid did not come from a normal pole (en.wikipedia and de.wikipedia).
- Not every weekend was on the ADAC GT Masters bill:
  - ADAC GT Masters: Oschersleben, Red Bull Ring, Nürburgring, Hockenheim (September).
  - DTM: Hockenheim (May) and Lausitzring (May). The official calendar marks both "mit DTM".
  - Formula One German Grand Prix: Hockenheim (July). Added on 26 March 2018. en.wikipedia calls it the first FIA F4 race on a European F1 weekend.
- 4 of the 7 weekends were at Hockenheim or with the DTM. The venues are Oschersleben, Hockenheim (three times), Lausitzring, Red Bull Ring and Nürburgring. The series did not race at Sachsenring, Zandvoort or Most in 2018.

## Points
- Every race: 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 for the top ten.
- No points for pole position or fastest lap (en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia).
- Guest drivers do not score.
- Zendeli's 348 points match his race results with this scale exactly.
- Separate rookie standings and team standings. The rookie table uses its own race positions (Schumacher scored 332 rookie points).

## Field
- 29 drivers started at least one race, from 9 entrants.
- 22 drivers scored points. Cordeel (rookie) started 5 races and scored 0. The official list shows only the 22 scorers.
- Guests not eligible for points: Ptáček (US Racing - CHRS, round 7), Salmenautio (KIC, rounds 1 and 5), Malvestiti and Saucy (Jenzer, round 5). en.wikipedia also lists Carrara (Jenzer) and Václav Šafář (privateer) as guests. de.wikipedia puts both in the standings with 0 points.
- Full-season grid was about 22 cars. Round 5 at Hockenheim had the largest field with the Jenzer cars and Šafář.

## Champion
- Lirim Zendeli (GER, US Racing - CHRS), 348 points, 10 wins from 20 races. He clinched the title after race 3 at the Nürburgring (round 6), with one weekend to go. He then won 2 more races at the finale.
- Liam Lawson second (234, 3 wins). Enzo Fittipaldi third (223, 1 win).
- Winners: Zendeli 10, Lawson 3, Vesti 2, Fittipaldi, Weerts, Wishofer, Caldwell, Krütten 1 each.
- Rookie champion: David Schumacher (332), ahead of Niklas Krütten (301).
- Teams: US Racing - CHRS 562, Van Amersfoort Racing 521, Prema Theodore Racing 416, ADAC Berlin-Brandenburg 275, Neuhauser Racing 104, KIC Driving Academy 51, KDC Racing 49 (official site; same on en.wikipedia and de.wikipedia).

## Drivers who later raced in DTM
- Liam Lawson: DTM in 2021 (en.wikipedia).
- David Schumacher: DTM in 2022 and 2023 (en.wikipedia).
- Leon Köhler: DTM debut late in 2022 for Walkenhorst Motorsport (BMW), replacing Esteban Muth (en.wikipedia).
- Mick Wishofer: DTM in 2023 for GRT Grasser Racing Team (en.wikipedia).
- Lirim Zendeli raced in the DTM Trophy support series in 2021, not in the DTM itself.
- Checked with no DTM found: Vesti, Weerts, Fittipaldi, Krütten, Caldwell, Doohan, Petecof, Cohen, Alders, Collet.
- Other later careers: Lawson and Doohan reached Formula One. Vesti, Fittipaldi, Collet, Caldwell, Zendeli and Cordeel raced in Formula 2 or Formula 3 (not checked in depth).

## Data notes and uncertainties
- Standings: the official ADAC page (archived), en.wikipedia and de.wikipedia agree on every points total, the rookie table and the team table.
- de.wikipedia gives 29 September as the season end. The official calendar and the race tables show 23 September.
- Round 2 and round 3 are marked as DTM weekends by en.wikipedia and by the official calendar. The game pack may want to treat these two as DTM support races and the rest as non-DTM.
- Nationality of Aaron di Comberti: en.wikipedia and driverdb British, de.wikipedia Spanish. British used.
- Tom Beckhäuser is entered as Czech by en.wikipedia and de.wikipedia. Other sources call him German-Czech.
- Birth dates: most are confirmed by two of en.wikipedia, de.wikipedia, driverdb and the official ADAC 2019 portraits. Single source or conflict:
  - Lucas Alecco Roy (driverdb only; ADAC news says he was 21 in 2018, which fits).
  - Aaron di Comberti and Sebastian Estner (driverdb only).
  - Tom Beckhäuser (EverybodyWiki only).
  - Konsta Lappalainen: en.wikipedia and driverdb say 11 August 2001, de.wikipedia says 2002. 2001 used.
  - Jesse Salmenautio: en.wikipedia and driverdb say 11 August 2001 (the same day as Lappalainen), 51gt3 says 10 August 2001, racingsportscars says 11 August 2002. Unverified.
  - Grégoire Saucy: en.wikipedia and motorsportstats say 26 December 1999, driverdb and older sources 29 December. 26 December used.
  - Václav Šafář: not found.
- Team colours: only US Racing - CHRS is photo-confirmed (one Commons photo). The Mücke-run ADAC Berlin-Brandenburg cars ran under the BWT Mücke Motorsport name, so pink is likely but not seen. Other teams left empty.
- Team bases are from earlier research or common knowledge (Van Amersfoort Huizen, Prema Grisignano di Zocco, Mücke Berlin, Jenzer Lyss, Neuhauser Zell am Ziller). The US Racing base was not found.
