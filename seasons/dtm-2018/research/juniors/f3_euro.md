# 2018 FIA Formula 3 European Championship

Research dossier for the DTM 2018 season pack. The series becomes a feeder series in the game.

2018 was the last season of the series. For 2019 it merged with GP3 into the new FIA Formula 3 Championship.

## Format

- 10 rounds, 30 races. 3 races each weekend. Usually race 1 on Saturday and races 2 and 3 on Sunday. Zandvoort, Silverstone and Hockenheim had races 1 and 2 on Saturday. Spa ran on Friday and Saturday.
- Car: Dallara for everyone (de.wiki lists the model as Dallara F317). Hankook tyres. Engines: Mercedes (Prema, Fortec, Van Amersfoort, Hitech) and Volkswagen (Motopark, Carlin, ma-con).
- Points, every race the same: 25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 1 for the top 10.
- No pole or fastest-lap bonus points in the en.wiki or de.wiki scoring tables.
- A race must run at least 25 minutes for full points. Pau race 3 was red-flagged in rain after 11 laps, so it paid half points.
- Guest drivers could not score points (Vesti at Hockenheim).
- Teams' championship: only the two best cars of each team scored in each race.
- Rookie Cup for drivers in their first F3 season.

## Field

- 27 drivers started at least one race: 26 classified in the championship plus 1 guest.
- 7 teams. Prema and Carlin ran up to 5 cars at once, Motopark 6, Hitech 4, Van Amersfoort 4.
- 21 full-season drivers. Part-season: Flörsch (rounds 4-10), Florescu (2-6), DeFrancesco (1-2), Hanses (ma-con 1-5, Carlin 9-10), Leong (6), Vesti (10, guest).
- ma-con (last seen 2013) and Fortec (last seen 2015) returned with one car each.

## Result

- Champion: Mick Schumacher (Prema Theodore Racing), 365 points, 8 wins. It was his second F3 season. He took the lead at the Red Bull Ring and clinched the title in the second Hockenheim race.
- Runner-up: Dan Ticktum (Motopark), 308 points, 4 wins.
- 3rd: Robert Shwartzman (Prema Theodore Racing), 294 points, 2 wins. Rookie champion.
- Other winners: Jüri Vips and Ralf Aron (4 each), Guanyu Zhou and Enaam Ahmed (2 each), Marcus Armstrong, Sacha Fenestraz, Jehan Daruvala and Nikita Troitskiy (1 each).
- Armstrong led the championship at half time. Ticktum led after Silverstone.

## 2018 links to DTM teams and makers

- 7 of the 10 rounds supported the DTM (Hungaroring, Norisring, Zandvoort, Misano, Nürburgring, Red Bull Ring, Hockenheim). Misano was new and replaced Monza.
- Joel Eriksson raced for Motopark in 2017 and moved to a BMW DTM seat for 2018. His Motopark seat went free.
- Maximilian Günther, 3rd in 2017 with Prema, was a Mercedes DTM junior. He moved to Formula 2 for 2018.
- Aberdein took part in an Audi DTM rookie test at the Lausitzring in 2017.
- 4 of the 7 teams used Mercedes engines. Not verified in this research: these were HWA-built engines (HWA also ran the Mercedes DTM programme in 2018).
- Motopark, Carlin and ma-con used Volkswagen engines. VW had no DTM programme. Its group sister Audi did.
- Driver programmes: Shwartzman, Armstrong and Zhou were Ferrari Driver Academy members (en.wiki season article). Ticktum was a Red Bull junior (en.wiki entry list source). No Audi, BMW or Mercedes junior status was confirmed for any 2018 driver.

## Nationality notes

Nationality follows the flag in the en.wiki standings. Licence differences:

- Marcus Armstrong is a New Zealander but raced on an Italian licence.
- Sebastián Fernández is Venezuelan-Spanish and raced on a Spanish licence. formel3guide lists him as VEN.
- Marino Sato is Japanese but raced on a San Marino licence.
- Keyvan Andres (Andres Soori) is German-Iranian and raced on an Iranian licence. formel3guide lists him as GER.
- Ameya Vaidyanathan raced on a British licence at rounds 1-3.

## Gaps and uncertainties

- Rounds started vs entered: the en.wiki results grid shows Florescu withdrew at round 7, DeFrancesco at rounds 3-4 and Hanses (ma-con) at round 6. The entry tables differ: en.wiki says Florescu 2-7, DeFrancesco 1-4, Hanses 1-6; de.wiki says Florescu 2-8 and Hanses 1-5. The CSV uses the rounds actually started.
- Chassis: en.wiki lists a chassis number per car (F312/0xx to F318/0xx). de.wiki gives Dallara F317 for every car. The CSV uses Dallara F317. en.wiki gives Florescu chassis F318/007, the same number as Ticktum. Not resolved.
- Team bases come from current en.wiki team pages. ma-con is known by country only. The Van Amersfoort (Zeewolde) and Hitech (Silverstone) bases may differ for 2018.
- Liveries: Van Amersfoort and Fortec have no colours found. Carlin is based on one car (Fenestraz) with a personal sponsor livery, so low confidence. The rest are estimated from Norisring 2018 photos on Wikimedia Commons. The photos are dull and the hex values were brightened by eye.
- Birth dates: Nikita Troitskiy has only one source (en.wiki). Ben Hingeley has no Wikipedia page; his date comes from driverdb and the FIA results database (seen in a search snippet, page not opened). driverdb gives 1999-03-25 for Mick Schumacher; en.wiki and de.wiki both give 1999-03-22, which is used.
- Standings: en.wiki and de.wiki agree on all 26 classified drivers and all 7 teams. motorsport-magazin.com agrees on the 22 point scorers.
