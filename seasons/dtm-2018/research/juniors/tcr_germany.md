# ADAC TCR Germany Touring Car Championship 2018

## Which series this is
- Third season of the ADAC's TCR touring car series (en.wikipedia). It replaced the ADAC Procar series, which the 2013 pack used as its touring car feeder (tw_cup files).
- de.wikipedia has a series article but no 2018 season article. The 2018 page is a red link there.
- Not a DTM support series. All 7 rounds ran on the ADAC GT Masters bill. ADAC Formula 4 shared 4 of these weekends (Oschersleben, Red Bull Ring, Nürburgring, Hockenheim).

## Cars
- TCR rules: front-wheel drive touring cars built by many makes to one set of rules, with Balance of Performance.
- Makes in the 2018 field (official entry lists and en.wikipedia):
  - Volkswagen Golf GTI TCR (2018 spec; Steibel ran a 2016 spec car)
  - Audi RS 3 LMS TCR
  - Cupra León TCR (SEAT)
  - Honda Civic Type R TCR (new FK8; Marcel Fugel used the older FK2, listed as "Honda Civic TCR 2017")
  - Opel Astra TCR
  - Hyundai i30 N TCR (Engstler team from round 4)
  - Peugeot 308 TCR (Niedertscheider only)
  - Renault Mégane RS TCR (Wolf-Power, Besagroup and guests)
- Engines in the entries file are the standard engines of each TCR model (VW and Cupra 2.0 TSI, Audi 2.0 TFSI, Honda 2.0 VTEC Turbo, Hyundai 2.0 T-GDi, Opel 1.6 turbo, Peugeot 1.6 THP, Renault 1.8 turbo). They are not checked car by car.
- Tyres: Hankook (stickers on every car in the Zandvoort photos).

## Classes
- No classes. One overall drivers' table and one teams' table.
- A sub-classification for rookies, the "Honda Rookie Challenge", ran inside the main race. Six drivers took part: Hesse, Wankmüller, Michelle Halder, Häußermann, Prattes and Marcel Fugel. Hesse won it with 98.5 points (official site).
- Guest starters ("Gaststarter") raced in the same race but could not score. They did not block points places: points went by position among eligible drivers.

## Format
- 7 weekends with 2 races each, 14 races. Race 1 on Saturday, race 2 on Sunday.
- 4 rounds in Germany and one each in the Czech Republic (Most, first visit), Austria and the Netherlands.
- Oschersleben race 2 was red-flagged. Less than 75% of the distance was run, so half points were given.

## Points
- Race points to the top 20 eligible finishers: 40-36-32-29-26-23-20-18-16-14-12-10-8-7-6-5-4-3-2-1 (en.wikipedia).
- Qualifying for race 1 gave 5-4-3-2-1 to the top five. en.wikipedia marks these as small numbers next to the race 1 results.
- No points for fastest lap. en.wikipedia lists a fastest-lap point and a 5-point pole bonus in its table, but the official totals only add up without the fastest-lap point.
- Check: Proczyk 421 and Langeveld 403 both match the official totals exactly with this system (race points by eligible position, half points at Oschersleben race 2, qualifying points for race 1).

## Field
- 39 drivers started at least one race: 29 point-eligible drivers and 10 guests.
- 28 drivers were classified with points. Steve Kirsch was eligible but only started one race (retired) and was not classified.
- Guests: Petr Fulín, Jan Seyffert, Daniel Davidovac, "Tessitore", Pascal Eberle, Leonardo Tinland, Francesco Ruga, Julien Apothéloz, Dino Calcum, Olli Kangas.
- Entered but never started, so left out of the entries file: Franjo Kovač (#15 Besagroup Renault, round 4, did not start; en.wikipedia flags him Croatian, the official entry list says DEU) and Milenko Vuković (#50 Wolf-Power Renault, round 3, did not start and withdrew).
- About 26 to 31 cars per weekend. The Red Bull Ring race 1 had 29 starters (official result sheet).
- Reigning teams' champion Target Competition left for TCR Europe.

## Results
- Champion: Harald Proczyk (AUT, HP Racing International, Opel Astra TCR), 421 points, 3 wins. He also runs the team. He took the title at the Hockenheim finale with a win in race 1 and third in race 2.
- Luca Engstler second with 414 points, 7 behind. He started in a VW Golf and moved to the Hyundai i30 N from round 4. He won 3 races, all with Hyundai.
- Niels Langeveld third (403, 2 wins). Mike Halder fourth (366, 3 wins).
- Race winners: Proczyk 3, Engstler 3, Mike Halder 3, Langeveld 2, Buri 1, Dominik Fugel 1, Michelle Halder 1. Guest Petr Fulín crossed the line first in Most race 2. Michelle Halder got the points win.
- Honda Rookie Challenge: Max Hesse 98.5, Luke Wankmüller 94, Michelle Halder 78.5, Justin Häußermann 42, Loris Prattes 30, Marcel Fugel 15 (official).

## DTM links
- Luca Engstler: raced in the DTM from 2023 (en.wikipedia lists 2023, 2024 and 2026 seasons).
- Peter Terting: raced in the DTM in 2003 for Abt Sportsline (Audi), before this season. He made a one-weekend appearance here at the Nürburgring.
- Checked with no DTM found: Proczyk, Langeveld, Mike Halder, Michelle Halder, Buri, Hesse, Leuchter, Reicher, Fulín.
- Kai Jordan, Kankkunen, Coicaud, Thoma, Dominik Fugel and the other drivers without Wikipedia articles were not checked in depth.

## Gaps and uncertainties
- Standings: official ADAC table (archived) and en.wikipedia agree on every driver total. de.wikipedia has no season page. en.wikipedia shows Prattes as position "29" (a typo for 19).
- Team table: en.wikipedia and the official site differ completely. Official values used.
- Race dates: en.wikipedia gives one date for both races at Red Bull Ring, Nürburgring, Zandvoort, Sachsenring and Hockenheim. TouringCarTimes reports show race 1 on the Saturday at each of these. Hockenheim race 1 on 22 September is inferred from that pattern and the ADAC news title.
- Birth dates: two sources (Wikipedia plus driverdb or racingsportscars) for Proczyk, Engstler, Langeveld, Mike Halder, Buri, Hesse, Leuchter, Michelle Halder, Reicher, Terting, Fulín, Niedertscheider, Seyffert and Kangas. All other dates are from driverdb only.
- Mike Halder: de.wikipedia and racingsportscars say 2 May 1996, driverdb says 5 February 1996. 2 May used.
- Birth dates not found: Justin Häußermann, "Tessitore" (pseudonym), Leonardo Tinland, Francesco Ruga.
- J.C. Reynolds: entered as Swiss on the official entry list and en.wikipedia. driverdb says Venezuelan. Swiss used.
- Olli Kangas: official entry list name. en.wikipedia uses "Olli Parhankangas" in its entry table and "Olli Kangas" in its results table.
- Alex Morgan: en.wikipedia lists his round 6 Besagroup entry as a guest, but counts his round 6 results in his standings row. He scored only at round 1.
- Team colours: from one set of Zandvoort photos (August 2018). Honda ADAC Sachsen, PROsport, Halder, Positione, Steibel and the Engstler VW cars were not seen. YACO and Niedertscheider are low confidence.
- Team bases are country level only, from en.wikipedia flags, except Engstler (Wiggensbach) and Halder (Meßkirch).
