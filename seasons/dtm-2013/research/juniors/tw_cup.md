# ADAC Procar 2013 (the later "Deutscher Tourenwagen Cup")

## Which series this is
- The series is the **ADAC Procar Serie**. Its official 2013 name was **"ADAC PROCAR - Deutsche Tourenwagen Challenge des DMSB"**. From 2012 the DMSB gave it back the old DTC (Deutsche Tourenwagen Challenge) title (de.wikipedia).
- In 2016 it was renamed **Deutscher Tourenwagen Cup (DTC)**, with the DMSB title "DMSB Tourenwagen Cup". The series stopped after 2016, and the 2017 season was cancelled.
- en.wikipedia files the whole history under "Deutscher Tourenwagen Cup". The line runs: DTC (Deutsche Tourenwagen Challenge) from 1995, then DMSB Produktionswagen-Meisterschaft (2004-2005), then ADAC Procar from 2006, then DTC again from 2016.
- **Why this one:** it is the only German touring-car series that carried both the "DTC" and the "Deutscher Tourenwagen Cup" names, and in 2013 it ran under the DTC title. It was the 19th ADAC Procar season counted by en.wikipedia.
- **Runner-up candidate:** the DMV Touring Car Championship / DMV Touring Car Challenge (DMV TCC). It ran in 2013 at club events (AvD and DMV meetings: Hockenheim, Red Bull Ring, Dijon, Monza). Its grid was a mixed club field that even included a Corvette GT3, so it is a weaker match for the name. Other candidates were not used. The VW Scirocco R-Cup was a one-make DTM support cup with no "Tourenwagen Cup" name.
- **Not a DTM support series.** All 8 rounds ran on the **ADAC Masters Weekend**, alongside the ADAC GT Masters, German F3 and ADAC Formel Masters.

## Format
- 8 weekends with 2 races each, so 16 races scheduled. Each race was about 50-65 km. At Oschersleben both races were on the Sunday, and Wikipedia gives only one date for every weekend.
- 5 rounds in Germany and one each in Belgium, Austria and Slovakia.
- **Points per race, top 8 in each division:** 10-8-6-5-4-3-2-1. **No** bonus points for pole or fastest lap (de.wikipedia).
- Divisions 1 and 2 shared the track but were **scored separately**. There is no overall drivers' classification.
- Slovakia race 1 was stopped after 2 laps when David Griessner rolled and damaged the barrier. It was not restarted and **not scored** (de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia lists Leidinger as the winner).
- Hockenheim race 2 in Division 1: Leidinger won on the road but was disqualified, and per de.wikipedia no one was moved up. en.wikipedia does move drivers up, which explains the two-point gaps for Huggler and Winkelhock.

## Classes
- **Division 1:** Super 2000 touring cars, as used in the WTCC. From 2011 the rules also allowed 1.6 turbo engines, but every 2013 starter drove a BMW (320si E90, or a 320i E46 for Huggler).
- **Division 2:** Super 1600 cars (de.wikipedia; TouringCarTimes uses "S1600"). The field was Ford Fiesta (1.6 16V and "ST" versions), Peugeot 207 Sport, Citroën Saxo VTS and Citroën C2 VTS.
- Division 3 was dropped in 2011. A Mini Trophy division was added in 2014.

## Field
- **28 drivers started:** 11 in Division 1 and 17 in Division 2.
- Two more entries are listed only on en.wikipedia and were DNS at every race, so they are left out of the entries file: Milenko Vukovic (#8, Vukovic Motorsport, BMW 320si) and Steffen Großmann (#49, Rennsportteam Großmann, Fiesta ST).
- Division 1 had only 3 to 5 cars per weekend. Liqui Moly Team Engstler ran a "press and VIP car" with a different driver each round: Bräutigam, Klien, Naumann, Kuhn, Warnecke, Schrick and Winkelhock.
- 10 entrant teams started.

## Results
- **Division 1 champion:** Jens-Guido Weimann (GER, Thate Motorsport, BMW 320si E90), 106 points. This was his second title in a row.
- Johannes Leidinger (Engstler) was second with 92 points and 7 wins on the road plus the Hockenheim race-2 win he lost. He had led the points before Hockenheim. The stewards excluded him from both races there after he collided with Weimann in race 1. Engstler appealed but withdrew the appeal on the eve of the hearing (TouringCarTimes, 25 Nov 2013).
- **Division 2 champion:** David Griessner (AUT, ETH Tuning, Peugeot 207 Sport), 104 points. He was a rookie and won 9 races.
- **Teams:** Thate Motorsport won Division 1 (106 points). ETH Tuning 1 won Division 2 (132 points).

## DTM links
- **Thomas Winkelhock** (Hockenheim guest, won race 1) raced in the DTM in 1990 for the AMG Mercedes works team and again in 1992 (de.wikipedia).
- Christian Klien (Spa guest) is a former F1 driver but has no DTM career.
- No other 2013 starter could be confirmed as a DTM driver before or after 2013.

## Gaps and uncertainties
- Team colours were not verified for any team, so those fields are left empty.
- Team bases are known only for Engstler (Wiggensbach).
- The exact engine for the Division 2 "Fiesta ST" cars is not documented. The road car has a 2.0 engine, but the class is Super 1600.
- Birth dates are missing for Reiner Kuhn, Norbert Heinz, Norbert Fuchs and Oleksandr Danylchenko. Most dates come from driverdb.
- The two Wikipedias disagree on some rounds and cars. The entries file follows de.wikipedia, whose results table is consistent with its entry list. The differences are listed in each row's notes.
- Saturday/Sunday split per weekend: only Oschersleben is confirmed as Sunday-only.
