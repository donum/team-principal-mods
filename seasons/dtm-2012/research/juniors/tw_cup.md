# ADAC Procar 2012 (the later "Deutscher Tourenwagen Cup")

## Which series this is
- The series is the **ADAC Procar Serie**. From 2012 the DMSB gave it back the old DTC (Deutsche Tourenwagen Challenge) title, so the official name was **"ADAC PROCAR - Deutsche Tourenwagen Challenge des DMSB"** (de.wikipedia, ADAC-Procar-Serie). 2012 was the first season under this title.
- In 2016 it was renamed **Deutscher Tourenwagen Cup (DTC)**. The series stopped after 2016.
- en.wikipedia counts 2012 as the 18th season of the ADAC Procar Series and calls it "the German championship for Super 2000 touring cars".
- The same series is used for 2013 (see the dtm-2013 tw_cup dossier), so the file id stays `tw_cup`.
- **Not a DTM support series.** Seven of the 8 rounds ran on the **ADAC Masters Weekend**, alongside the ADAC GT Masters, German F3 and ADAC Formel Masters. Round 4 at Oschersleben (6-8 July) was a club meeting called **"Preis der Stadt Magdeburg"**, run by Motorsport Club Stuttgart with the ATS Formel 3 Cup, NEC Formel Renault 2.0 and DMV TCC (event entry document and SDO timing sheets, single source each). The ADAC GT Masters raced at the Nürburgring that July weekend instead.

## Format
- 8 weekends with 2 races each, so 16 races. Each race was about 37-52 km.
- 7 different tracks: Oschersleben hosted two rounds. 6 rounds in Germany and one each in the Netherlands and Austria.
- **Points per race, top 8 in each division:** 10-8-6-5-4-3-2-1. **No** bonus points for pole or fastest lap (de.wikipedia).
- Divisions 1 and 2 shared the track but were **scored separately**. There is no overall drivers' classification.
- **Teams' scoring:** only the best-placed car of a team scores in each race. This explains why Thate has 158 and not 146 + 34. Checked by hand: Weimann's 12 wins in rounds 1-6 plus Leidinger's 8 + 10 at the Nürburgring plus Weimann's two Hockenheim wins make 158. ETH Tuning: Thierfelder 137 plus 7 extra from Rinke in Hockenheim race 2 makes 144.
- Wikipedia gives only one date per weekend (the Sunday). The calendar uses that date for both days, as in the 2013 file.

## Classes
- **Only two divisions in 2012.** Division 3 was dropped after 2011 (de.wikipedia, ADAC-Procar-Serie).
- **Division 1:** Super 2000 touring cars. Starters drove the BMW 320si E90 (Thate, Engstler, KM Racing, Vukovic), the BMW 320i E46 (Huggler), one Audi A4 Super 2000 (K & K Motorsport) and one Honda Civic FD (Rikli).
- **Division 2:** Super 1600 cars. TouringCarTimes says Division 2 was "based on the S1600 class". The field was Ford Fiesta (1.6 16V, ST, VCT Sport and TI-VCT versions), Chevrolet Aveo, Peugeot 207 Sport, Citroën Saxo VTS and Citroën C2 VTS.

## Field
- **30 drivers started:** 8 in Division 1 and 22 in Division 2.
- One more entry is left out of the entries file because he did not start either race: Vadim Meshcheriakov (#36, Maurer Motorsport, Zandvoort). driverdb gives him as Russian, born 1991-11-25.
- Division 1 had only 2 to 4 cars per weekend. Thate added a second car for reigning champion Leidinger for the last two rounds (TouringCarTimes, 10 Sep 2012).
- **17 entrant teams**: 7 ran Division 1 cars and 11 ran Division 2 cars (Engstler ran both). ATM Racing only ran one guest start for Borgnino, whose points went to Maurer Motorsport.

## Results
- **Division 1 champion:** Jens-Guido Weimann (GER, Thate Motorsport, BMW 320si E90), 146 points. His first title. He won 14 of 16 races and clinched the title at the Lausitzring (TouringCarTimes, 27 Aug 2012). He was 3rd in Nürburgring race 1 and did not start race 2.
- Paul Green (Liqui Moly Team Engstler) was second with 91 points, 55 behind (TouringCarTimes, 30 Sep 2012).
- **Division 2 champion:** Guido Thierfelder (GER, ETH Tuning, Peugeot 207 Sport), 137 points. His fifth title. He clinched it early at the Nürburgring. He won 12 of 16 races.
- Lennart Marioneck (Engstler) and Ralf Glatzel tied on 87 points. Marioneck is 2nd on wins (4 to 0). ADAC also names Marioneck 2nd and Glatzel 3rd.
- **Teams:** Thate Motorsport won Division 1 (158 points). ETH Tuning 1 won Division 2 (144 points). Engstler was 2nd in both (91 and 98).
- Champions confirmed by three sources: de.wikipedia, en.wikipedia and TouringCarTimes. ADAC Motorsport confirms both names.

## DTM links
- No 2012 Procar starter raced in the DTM in 2012. The 2012 DTM grid in `seasons/dtm-2012/research/entries.csv` has no shared names.
- No starter could be confirmed as a DTM driver before or after 2012.
- Engstler Motorsport is a touring-car team (WTCC, later TCR). It has no DTM entry in 2012 or 2013.
- Thomas Winkelhock (DTM 1990 and 1992) only appears in the 2013 season, not in 2012.

## Links to the 2013 season
- Weimann (Division 1 champion again in 2013), Leidinger (moved back to Engstler for 2013), Huggler, Vukovic (entered in 2013 but did not start), Thierfelder, Rinke, Heinz, Trampert, Reinsberger, Krauchuk, Glatzel, Mierschke and Griessner all raced in both seasons.
- David Griessner did 3 rounds for Glatzel in 2012 and won the 2013 Division 2 title as a rookie with ETH Tuning.

## Gaps and uncertainties
- Team colours were not verified for any team, so those fields are left empty.
- Team bases are known only for Engstler (Wiggensbach).
- **"Paul Green":** de.wikipedia writes the name in quotation marks and says it was his first motorsport season. That suggests a pseudonym, but no source confirms it. No birth date found. A Paul Green also won a Gentlemen's class race in the 2013 ADAC GT Masters (BMW Z4 GT3, Vita4One). It is not proven to be the same person.
- Birth dates are missing for Paul Green, Michael Meyer, Norbert Heinz, Alexander Frolov, Bodo Cordes, René Freisberg, Ingo Wirtz, Michael Zeilinger, René Keyselt and Patrick Rahn.
- Erwin Lukas: the driverdb date (1958-01-28) is from a page that shows ETCC Super 1600 seasons but no 2012 Procar line. Likely the same man (he drove a Fiesta for GENA, a Super 1600 team), but not confirmed.
- Nationalities of the drivers without a driverdb page come from the Wikipedia flags only.
- en.wikipedia gives longer round lists for Kast, Vukovic, Wirtz, Krammes, Janning, Frolov and Meshcheriakov. The entries file follows the de.wikipedia results table, which matches its points. The differences are in each row's notes.
- en.wikipedia gives Griessner 24 points, de.wikipedia 23. His de.wikipedia results (5, 4, 3, 3, Ret, 7) add up to 23.
- The car of Frolov (#37) and Meshcheriakov (#36) is listed as the Chevrolet Aveo by both Wikipedias, but no race report confirms it.
- The Saturday/Sunday split per weekend is not known. The Oschersleben July timing sheets number both Procar races as "Rennen 17-1" and "17-2", which hints that both ran on the same day.
- Round 4 venue: Wikipedia calls it Oschersleben for both rounds. The track is the same as round 1, so the same venue name is used.
