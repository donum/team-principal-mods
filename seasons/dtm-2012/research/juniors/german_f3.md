# German F3 2012 (ATS Formel 3 Cup)

The 2012 ATS Formel 3 Cup was the German Formula 3 championship (10th season under the "F3 Cup" name, 38th German F3 season by de.wikipedia's count). Organiser: Formel-3-Vereinigung e.V. (F3V). Title sponsor ATS (wheels). SONAX sponsored the rookie cup ("SONAX Rookie-Pokal"); DEKRA logos sit on every number board.

2012 was the first year of the one-make Volkswagen Power Engine (about 220 hp plus a push-to-pass button worth about 20 hp, roughly ten uses per race, per de.wikipedia). The Dallara F308 generation was allowed for the first time. Most cars ran as "Dallara F311", which is an F308 with an update kit.

## Format

- 9 race weekends, 3 races each, 27 races. All 27 were held.
- Normal weekend: races 1 and 2 on Saturday, race 3 on Sunday. Assen had one race per day (Friday to Sunday).
- 5 rounds in Germany, 2 in the Netherlands (Zandvoort, Assen), 1 in Belgium (Spa), 1 in Austria (Red Bull Ring).
- Spa and Assen were "invitation rounds" (en.wikipedia footnote). Points still counted.
- Support package: 6 rounds on the ADAC Masters Weekend with ADAC GT Masters (Zandvoort, Sachsenring, Red Bull Ring, Lausitzring, Nürburgring, Hockenheim; dates match the 2012 ADAC GT Masters calendar). Oschersleben (7-8 July) was the "Preis der Stadt Magdeburg" club meeting run by MCS Stuttgart (ADAC Procar, NEC Formel Renault 2.0 and others). en.wikipedia counts seven ADAC Masters Weekends, but GT Masters raced Oschersleben on 30 March, not in July. Spa supported the International Superstars Series per en.wikipedia (de.wikipedia says International GT Open). Assen was the GAMMA Race Day.
- No round was on the DTM bill in 2012.
- Nürburgring replaced Zolder from the first calendar (de.wikipedia).
- Grids (de.wikipedia, citing Motorsport-Total): race 1 grid from each driver's fastest qualifying lap, race 3 grid from the second-fastest lap. Race 2 grid: top 8 of race 1 reversed, the rest in race 1 order.

| Race 1 | 25 | 18 | 15 | 12 | 10 | 8 | 6 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 3 | 3 |

| Race 3 | 25 | 18 | 15 | 12 | 10 | 8 | 6 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 3 | 3 |

So bonus points: pole for races 1 and 3 (3 each), fastest lap in every race (3). This is different from 2013 (pole for race 1 only, fastest lap 2). de.wikipedia says bonus points went only to Cup-class drivers. Eriksson clinched the title with pole points in Hockenheim qualifying (formulascout via en.wikipedia).

### Classes

- **Cup** (main title): only 2008-2011 cars (F308/F310/F311) with the Volkswagen Power Engine scored Cup points. Unlike 2013, Trophy drivers do not appear in the Cup table.
- **Trophy**: 2002-2007 cars with conventional F3 engines (Opel, Opel OPC-Challenge, older Volkswagen). Mostly gentleman drivers and young Germans on a budget.
- **SONAX Rookie-Pokal**: won by Lucas Auer (414) ahead of Gilbert (399) and Markelov (291). The rookie table also lists Stolz, van de Laar, Rudersdorf, Siddiqi, Bryant-Meisner and Hackl.

They were not in the Cup table; I gave them 0 points and ordered them by Trophy result so the game can read them. Their Trophy points are in the notes. Aberer (guest) has an empty pos.

## Field size

- 22 drivers started at least one race (15 in Cup cars only, 6 in Trophy cars only, Luca Stolz in both). Zoël Amberg was entered once (Red Bull Ring) but did not start, so 23 drivers were entered.
- 12 entrant names. ADM Motorsport's only starter was Alon Day.
- Grid size roughly 15 to 21 cars per weekend (estimate from the results tables; Hockenheim race 3 had 15 starters per the official result).
- All cars Dallara. Cup cars: F308, F310 or F311 with the Volkswagen Power Engine. Trophy cars: F302 to F306 (de.wikipedia calls several of them F307) with Opel, Opel OPC-Challenge or older Volkswagen engines.

## Results

- **Champion: Jimmy Eriksson** (SWE, Lotus / Motopark), 408 points, 8 wins in 27 races. Verified on en.wikipedia, de.wikipedia and the official F3V press release from Hockenheim race 27. He was the first Swedish German F3 champion.
- 2nd Lucas Auer 298 (rookie, Van Amersfoort Racing), 3rd Kimiya Sato 281 (Lotus).
- Trophy champion: André Rudersdorf (ma-con, Volkswagen), 377 Trophy points, ahead of Jordi Weckx 300 and Dominik Kocher 268. Luca Stolz led the Trophy fight early but moved up to the Cup class from Assen.
- Race winners: Eriksson 8, Blomqvist 5, Sato 4, Binder 3, Gilbert 2, Auer 2, Markelov 2, Mettler 1 (27 races; en.wikipedia and de.wikipedia agree).
- Teams standings: not found. Neither Wikipedia page has a teams table and no official PDF was found. A simple sum of Cup points per entrant (not an official table) would be Lotus 874, Van Amersfoort 617, Performance Racing 517, EuroInternational about 233, ADM about 40.

## Later careers / DTM links

- **Lucas Auer** (2nd): DTM driver for Mercedes from 2015 (en.wikipedia lists DTM seasons 2015-2026). Nephew of Gerhard Berger, who later ran the DTM (ITR chairman from 2017, general knowledge).
- **Tom Blomqvist** (5th): BMW DTM driver 2015-2017 (en.wikipedia).
- **Luca Stolz** (10th, Trophy then Cup): Mercedes-AMG DTM driver 2021-2024 (en.wikipedia).
- **Daniel Abt** (guest at Spa): never raced in the DTM (en.wikipedia), but his family team Abt Sportsline ran Audi Sport Team Abt and Audi Sport Team Abt Sportsline in the 2012 DTM.
- Checked en.wikipedia for Eriksson, Sato, Gilbert, Binder, Markelov, Mettler, van de Laar, Day, Siddiqi, Bryant-Meisner, Cerruti: no DTM.
- Cross-check with seasons/dtm-2012/research/entries.csv and seasons/dtm-2013/research/entries.csv: none of this field raced in the 2012 or 2013 DTM.
- Markelov, Siddiqi, Bryant-Meisner, Mettler, Weckx, Iannaccone, Hackl and Aberer also appear in the 2013 German F3 file.

## Links to DTM makers and teams in 2012

- **Engines:** Volkswagen powered every Cup car. VW was not a DTM maker in 2012 (its group brand Audi was). No Mercedes or BMW engines in the 2012 field.
- **Abt:** Daniel Abt drove one weekend for Van Amersfoort. Abt Sportsline (Kempten) ran two Audi DTM teams in 2012.
- **Lotus / Motopark:** the top team ran under the Lotus name (black and gold). No DTM link in 2012. Mücke Motorsport (Mercedes DTM team) was not in German F3 in 2012.
- No shared weekends with the DTM in 2012. Red Bull Ring, Lausitzring, Nürburgring, Hockenheim, Oschersleben and Zandvoort were also DTM venues that year, on different dates.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Spa support series: International Superstars Series (en.wikipedia) vs International GT Open (de.wikipedia). The 2012 Superstars Series did race at Spa; GT Open not checked.
- Oschersleben support: en.wikipedia calls it ADAC Masters Weekend; the event documents name "Preis der Stadt Magdeburg" (MCS Stuttgart). Used the event name.
- Aberer: en.wikipedia and formel3guide give car #68 and guest status with 0 points; the official Hockenheim race 3 result shows #68. de.wikipedia gives #69 and scores him 7th in the Trophy with 41. Used #68 and guest.
- Iannaccone Trophy points: 252 (en.wikipedia) vs 247 (de.wikipedia). He withdrew from round 8 (WD in en.wikipedia results); both entry lists say all rounds. Used rounds 1-7 and 9.
- Weckx: en.wikipedia entry list says all rounds; results and de.wikipedia show rounds 1-8. Used 1-8.
- Gilbert birth date: 1994-05-10 (formel3guide, de.wikipedia) vs 1995-05-10 (en.wikipedia). Used 1994.
- Single-source birth dates (formel3guide only): Dominik Kocher 1991-10-11, Sylvain Warnecke 1979-12-30.
- Chassis: en.wikipedia gives serials (used). de.wikipedia shows all Cup cars as "Dallara F311" and most Trophy cars as F307. The official result PDF also names Kocher's and Aberer's cars "F 307".
- Bryant-Meisner and Stolz share chassis F310/002 in en.wikipedia, so Stolz likely took over the car after Bryant-Meisner's injury (inference).
- Team bases: only Motopark (Oschersleben) and Van Amersfoort (Huizen) given as towns (reused from the 2013 file); others are country only (flag in en.wikipedia).
- Liveries: read from one formel3guide photo per car; Van Amersfoort and Franz Wöss had mixed liveries (see german_f3_teams.csv).
- Teams standings and race lengths not found.
