# 2012 Formula 3 Euro Series

Research dossier for the DTM 2012 season pack. The series becomes a feeder series in the game, in the same `f3_euro` slot that the FIA Formula 3 European Championship fills in 2013.

## Which series this is

- 2012 was the 10th and last season of the Formula 3 Euro Series. From 2013 it was replaced by the FIA Formula 3 European Championship.
- In 2012 the FIA also ran a revived FIA Formula 3 European Championship. It had 10 rounds with 2 counted races each. All 8 Euro Series weekends were FIA rounds too. The other 2 FIA rounds were British F3 weekends at Pau and Spa-Francorchamps (en.wikipedia).
- So the FIA championship and the Euro Series are mostly the same field. Juncadella won both: 240 points in the Euro Series and 252 in the FIA table.
- At Euro Series weekends the FIA table counted 2 of the 3 races. The FIA "race 2" at the Norisring is the race that was red-flagged, which is Euro Series race 3. So the FIA table most likely counted Euro Series races 1 and 3 and skipped race 2. This is inferred, not stated in a source.

### Base used: Euro Series standings

The files use the **Euro Series** standings, not the FIA table, because:

- Every round was on the DTM bill, so the series fits the game's DTM calendar. The FIA table adds Pau and Spa, which were British F3 weekends with a different field.
- The FIA table mixes two organisers. British F3 regulars were guests at Euro rounds and Euro regulars were guests at British rounds, so points eligibility changes from round to round.
- The Euro Series table counts all 24 races. The FIA table drops a race on each Euro weekend.
- The order of the top 10 is almost the same in both tables. Wehrlein (2nd) and Sainz (9th) move most: Sainz is 5th in the FIA table.

## Format

- 8 rounds, 24 races. 3 races each weekend. All rounds supported the DTM.
- The provisional calendar (25 November 2011) had 9 rounds. On 2 April 2012 the Lausitzring round was replaced by Brands Hatch and Oschersleben was dropped.
- Race 2 used a reversed grid (Autosport report of Marciello's Hockenheim race 2 win). The exact rule was not found.
- Car: Dallara F312 for most cars. Jo Zeller (Zeller) and two guest cars used the older F308. Engines: Mercedes (HWA-built, general knowledge), Volkswagen (Carlin, ma-con, Van Amersfoort), ThreeBond Nissan and Mugen-Honda (guest cars only).
- **Points scale: not found.** No source read gives the 2012 scale for races 1, 2 and 3, or any bonus points. Not verified.
- Half points: Norisring race 3 (red-flagged in heavy rain after half distance) and Zandvoort race 2 (only 5 laps in the time limit).
- Norisring race 1: Juncadella was disqualified for collisions with Wehrlein and Marciello. No driver was moved up, so the race has no official winner.
- Guest drivers (car numbers 30 and up) could not score points. Their teams were also not eligible for team points.

## Field

- 33 drivers started at least one race: 15 classified regulars and 18 guests.
- 8 point-scoring teams plus 4 guest-only teams (Fortec, Double R, T-Sport, Van Amersfoort). Carlin also ran 5 guest cars beside its 2 regulars.
- The Norisring round was shared with British F3, so most guests appeared there.

## Result

- **Champion: Daniel Juncadella** (ESP, Prema Powerteam), 240 points. Confirmed by en.wikipedia, formulascout and racecar.com. He also won the FIA European title (252) and the Masters of Formula 3.
- 2nd Pascal Wehrlein (Mücke), 226 points, rookie champion. 3rd Raffaele Marciello (Prema), 219.5.
- Wins per formulascout: Marciello 6, Juncadella 5, Rosenqvist 4, Buller 2, Wehrlein, Müller and Lewis 1 each. Guest Alexander Sims appears as a race winner at the Nürburgring in the en.wikipedia results table (uncertain reading).
- Teams: Prema Powerteam 609.5, Mücke Motorsport 441.5, Carlin 294.5, ma-con Motorsport 248.5, Jo Zeller Racing 38, URD Rennsport 36, Angola Racing Team 14, GU-Racing 0.
- Nations Cup: Germany.

## Drivers who later raced in DTM

- Daniel Juncadella: Mercedes from 2013 (Mücke in 2013). **In the 2013 DTM.**
- Pascal Wehrlein: Mercedes from 2013 (Mücke), DTM champion 2015. **In the 2013 DTM.**
- Felix Rosenqvist: Mercedes 2016.
- Tom Blomqvist: BMW 2015-2017.
- Lucas Auer (guest here): Mercedes from 2015.
- Philip Ellis: 2021 (Mercedes-AMG, Winward/HTP).
- None of this field is on the 2012 DTM grid (checked against research/entries.csv).

## 2012 links to DTM teams and makers

- Every round was a DTM support race.
- Mücke Motorsport ran Mercedes DTM cars and its F3 team in 2012. Both of its F3 drivers' paths (Wehrlein, and Juncadella from Prema) led to Mücke's DTM team in 2013.
- Most teams used Mercedes engines. VW powered Carlin and ma-con; VW had no DTM programme, but its sister brand Audi did.
- Driver programmes: Marciello was a Ferrari Driver Academy member, Sainz a Red Bull junior, Blomqvist a McLaren junior (formulascout). No BMW, Audi or Mercedes junior status was confirmed for 2012.

## Gaps and uncertainties

- Points scale per race and bonus points: not found.
- Wehrlein: formulascout and a season review give 226. One read of the en.wikipedia standings gave 229. 226 is used.
- Luís Sá Silva: en.wikipedia lists all rounds; formulascout says 7 of 8. The round he missed was not found.
- The en.wikipedia entry table splits team, chassis and engine cells. Goddard's team (T-Sport with a Mugen-Honda F308) is a reading of that table.
- Nick McBride: no birth date found. Speedcafe said he was 21 in February 2012.
- Single-source birth dates (driverdb only): van Asseldonk, Uhrhane, Ilyas, Padayachee.
- Philip Ellis: entered as British; born in Munich; en.wikipedia calls him Swiss-British-German.
- Félix Serrallés is Puerto Rican. In 2013 he raced on a US licence. His 2012 licence was not checked.
- Angola Racing Team: base and operator not found.
- Liveries: not checked for 2012. Prema, Mücke and Carlin colours are carried over from the 2013 file and marked low confidence. The rest are empty.
- Team bases come from the 2013 file.
