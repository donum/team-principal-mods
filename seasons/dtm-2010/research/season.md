# 2010 DTM: research dossier

## Overview
- 24th DTM season and the 11th since the 2000 relaunch. There were 11 rounds, from Hockenheim on 25 Apr to Shanghai on 28 Nov.
- One race per event. No qualifying race.
- Two makes: Audi (A4 DTM) and Mercedes-Benz (AMG C-Klasse W204). There were 19 entries plus one replacement driver. Team Kolles (Futurecom) had withdrawn, so Audi went from 12 cars to 9.
- Champion: **Paul di Resta** (HWA, AMG-Mercedes), 71 pts, ahead of Paffett (67) and Spengler (66). Best Audi driver was Scheider, 4th.
- New venues: Valencia (Circuit Ricardo Tormo) and the Shanghai street circuit replaced Zolder and the Sachsenring. Adria was added mid-season.
- First season with a season finale outside Germany since 2000.

## Regulations
- **Development freeze** for cost reasons. Only the 2008 and 2009 cars were used, unchanged from 2009.
  - Mercedes: HWA had 4 × 2009 cars. Persson and Mücke ran 2008 cars.
  - Audi: Abt's factory cars were 2009 spec. Phoenix, Rosberg and the Abt rookie car were 2008 spec.
- **Engines:** 4.0-litre 90° V8, naturally aspirated, about 460 hp (en.wiki car articles). Restrictor size not verified.
- **Weight:** 1,050 kg including driver for current cars (en.wiki Audi A4 DTM and W204 articles).
  - ru.wiki: older cars could run 5 kg lighter, at 1,025 kg against 1,050 kg. The success-ballast system was dropped for 2010 (auto-sport.ru, 2010-04-16). This is unverified.
- **Tyres:** Dunlop control tyre (SP Sport Maxx). It was Dunlop's last season. On 19 Nov 2010 Dunlop said it would not renew, and Hankook took over from 2011.
- **Qualifying:** knockout in four segments. Q4 was a single-lap shootout of the top 4.
  - The 2014 de.wiki article confirms that this Q4 "Einzelzeitfahren" existed until 2013.
  - ru.wiki says Q4 became a sequential single-lap super-pole in 2010 and the other three segments were shortened.
  - Exact segment lengths and how many drivers dropped out per segment are not verified.
- **Race:** one race of about 160–190 km per event. Two mandatory pit stops with tyre changes; refuelling was still allowed (en.wiki 2012: refuelling was banned only from 2012). A race time limit also applied (see 2011).
- **Points:** top 8 scored 10-8-6-5-4-3-2-1 (de.wiki). Team points were the sum of both cars. There was no official manufacturer table (one first appeared in 2012).

## Storylines
- Spengler led di Resta by up to 15 pts after four races. Di Resta then took the lead with three wins: Brands Hatch, Oschersleben and Hockenheim II.
- At Adria, Audi drivers Rockenfeller and Molina spun di Resta to 9th, and Spengler took a 3-point lead into the final.
- Shanghai finale: Paffett won from di Resta, who took the title. Spengler was 13th. The race was red-flagged early after a Stoddart/Rockenfeller crash and full points were given.
- David Coulthard made his DTM debut. Ralf Schumacher scored only 3 pts.
- Audi dropped Prémat before Shanghai and brought in Darryl O'Young.
- BMW's 2012 return was confirmed on 15 Oct 2010 at Hockenheim. Di Resta left for F1 (Force India).

## Unverified
- Lap counts disagree between sources:
  - pl.wiki gives Norisring 80, Nürburgring 48, Zandvoort 41 and Oschersleben 47.
  - racingyears and the de.wiki distances give 82/49/42/50. Those are used in calendar.csv, but they may be scheduled rather than completed laps.
- Adria: 38 laps × 2.702 km (pl.wiki, racingyears), but de.wiki gives 159.418 km (59 laps).
- Shanghai: 49 or 50 laps completed out of 60. The 2.465 km length comes from pl.wiki and de.wiki; racingyears gives 2.217 km.
- Hockenheim round 9 layout: 3.736 km, 47 laps. en.wiki calls it the "National Circuit" but the name is not confirmed.
- Lighter weight for older cars and the abolished success ballast come only from ru.wiki.
- Exact qualifying segment lengths and elimination numbers.
- The Audi engine was attributed to Neil Brown/Mugen on en.wiki. This looks dubious and was not used.
- The 2010 manufacturer title (Mercedes) has no points total.
- Team bases for Team Rosberg and Persson are left empty. Rosberg is believed to be Neustadt/Weinstraße, but this is unconfirmed for 2010.
- Darryl O'Young's nationality: en.wiki gives Hong Kong, de.wiki gives China.
