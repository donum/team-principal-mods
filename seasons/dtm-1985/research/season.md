# 1985 Deutsche Produktionswagen-Meisterschaft (DPM)

Second and last season under the DPM name (renamed DTM for 1986).

## Series & regulations
- ONS-sanctioned Group A touring cars with the displacement-based weight/tyre handicap from 1984 ("Group A with weight and tire penalties" per TCR). Base table: 1600 cc 750 kg / 2000 cc 880 kg / 2500 cc 960 kg / 3000 cc 1165 kg / 4000 cc 1365 kg / >4000 cc 1425 kg. +70 kg for multi-valve engines, up to +120 kg or narrower tyres for overly strong cars. Noise limit 100 dBA. The table is documented for 1984 and assumed to continue.
- New for 1985: the championship was open to international drivers, so foreign drivers scored points (unlike 1984).
- Points: 20-18-16-...-1 for the top 18. Two-heat events paid half points per heat. 1 drop result.
- Mid-season rule change: at Diepholz (round 6, July) the ONS decided to adjust compensation weights because the Volvos, Mustangs and Camaro outclassed the BMWs and Rover. The decision came late and was partly postponed: BMW and Rover could already shed 50 kg at Diepholz, while the Ford (Sierra) and Volvo weights were raised from the following races. Protests (Alfa Romeo, Ford) and an appeal over Diepholz were still open at the finale.
- Ford Sierras ran at 1365 kg (TCR Mainz report). Per TCR car sheets the DPM Volvo was 1165 kg ("later increased") and the Schnitzer DPM 635 was 1370 kg ("later reduced").

## Weekend format
- Timed practice sets the grid; no qualifying race. Standing starts. About 100 km sprints, no mandatory pit stops.
- Erding and Diepholz ran two heats (half points each).
- Venues: Zolder (x2), AVUS, Nürburgring, plus the airfields Wunstorf, Mainz-Finthen, Erding, Diepholz and Siegerland.

## Storylines
- The turbo era began. Volvo 240 Turbos (IPS/Stureson, Kissling/Peil, M&L/Andersson) and the V8s were the class of the field early on. Works-backed Ford Sierra XR4Tis arrived: Niedzwiedz won at the AVUS, then Klaus Ludwig won 4 of the last 5 races/heats after joining ABR Ringshausen.
- Team Schnitzer entered BMW 635s (Ravaglia, Berger, Oestreich) in early rounds, then withdrew. Ravaglia took pole at Zolder.
- Per Stureson (Volvo) won the title with 117.5 points and only one win (Wunstorf). Manthey (Rover) was 2nd on 100 after his Diepholz disqualifications, and Grohs (BMW 635) 3rd on 96. Peil's title chances ended when his car was destroyed in the Nürburgring warm-up.
- Former ski champion Franz Klammer joined Marko RSM (Alfa Romeo) from round 2. Manuel Reuter drove an HWRT Sierra from round 4 and led at Siegerland until the electrics failed. Beate Nodes drove a Ford Grab Escort RS Turbo.
