# 1996 International Touring Car Championship (ITC): research dossier

## Overview
- The DTM title was merged into the FIA ITC, which became the only championship. Official DTM statistics count it as a DTM season.
- 13 weekends × 2 heats = 26 races, held 14 April – 10 November:
  - 6 weekends in Germany
  - 5 elsewhere in Europe: Estoril, Helsinki, Silverstone, Magny-Cours, Mugello
  - 2 overseas: **Interlagos** (27 Oct) and **Suzuka** (10 Nov)
- Champion: **Manuel Reuter** (Joest Racing Opel Calibra V6 4x4) with 218 points. It was Opel's first DTM/ITC title and its biggest motorsport success since the 1982 rally world title (de.wiki ITC).
- Manufacturers: Opel 349, Alfa Romeo 340, Mercedes-Benz 305.
- Wins by make: Alfa Romeo 10, Opel 9, Mercedes 7. de.wiki calls it the first DTM-era season in which all three makes were evenly matched.
- This was the final season. On 25 September 1996, Alfa Romeo and Opel jointly announced their withdrawal because of costs. With Mercedes alone, the series folded; Mercedes/AMG moved to FIA GT. The DTM name returned in 2000.

## Class 1 regulations (FIA Class 1; from 1996 "Group CL1" per ja.wiki)
- Same base rules as 1995: 2.5 L, at most 6 cylinders, 4 valves per cylinder, naturally aspirated, production-derived block. 4WD, traction control, ABS and electronic differentials were allowed. The rev limit was 12,000 rpm from 1995.
- **1996 minimum weight: 1,040 kg regardless of drivetrain** (ja.wiki citing Racing On No. 485). This ended the 1995 2WD/4WD split of 1,020 / 1,060 kg.
- Drivetrains:
  - Opel Calibra V6 4x4: 4WD, Cosworth-built V6
  - Alfa Romeo 155 V6 TI: 4WD
  - AMG-Mercedes C-Klasse: RWD
- de.wiki calls 1996 the most technically advanced touring-car championship ever. Features included:
  - Fully automatic or semi-automatic gearboxes
  - Laptop-programmed differentials and anti-roll bars for each point of the track
  - Hydraulic ride height
  - Movable ballast sled
  - Air-intake shutters
  - Mercedes' quick-change front and rear modules
- Mercedes' engine change took about 10 minutes (motorsportretro).
- Engine output was about 450 bhp (motorsportretro).
- Opel used Williams-designed active suspension (motorsportretro).
- de.wiki cites an upper budget limit of about 51 million euro. The exact meaning of that figure is unclear.

## Points and format
- Points per heat for positions 1–10: 20-15-12-10-8-6-4-3-2-1. No bonus points.
- Manufacturer scoring method is not stated for 1996. In 1995 it was best car per make.
- Weekend:
  - Qualifying sets the heat 1 grid.
  - Two heats of about 100 km. Estoril and Diepholz heat 1 were shorter.
  - The **heat 2 grid is the heat 1 finishing order**, confirmed in en.wiki round classifications (Hockenheim-1: Reuter won heat 1 and started heat 2 from pole).
  - Both heats ran on the same day, 30 minutes apart, and crews had 22 minutes for repairs (motorsportretro).
- **Mandatory pit stops:** none found. The race notes (motorsport.com, Interlagos) mention only incidental stops for punctures and failures. Unverified.

## Overseas rounds
- **Interlagos (round 12):** run in wet/drying conditions with about 25,000 spectators.
  - Each make entered a local driver: Fittipaldi (Mercedes, replacing Thiim), Kanaan (Opel, replacing Wurz), Max Wilson (Alfa, replacing Watt; 2nd in heat 2).
  - Reuter clinched the title here.
- **Suzuka (round 13):** Japanese guest drivers were Aguri Suzuki (UPS Mercedes), Naoki Hattori (JAS Alfa) and Masanori Sekiya (Joest Opel).
  - Franchitti won heat 1 and Schneider heat 2.
- These long-haul trips to markets where some of the road cars were not sold helped drive costs up (en.wiki).

## Storylines
- Zakspeed switched from Mercedes to Opel, running Alzen and Ludwig.
- Team Rosberg ran Stuck and Lehto after Keke Rosberg retired as a driver.
- Opel sporting chief Walter Treser was replaced by Wolfgang-Peter Flohr (de.wiki Calibra).
- Nannini took 7 wins, including four in a row mid-season (Nürburgring-2 ×2 and Magny-Cours ×2; he also won both Estoril heats). He was hurt by retirements and failed to score 12 times.
- Reuter won on consistency: he scored in the first 15 races and failed to score only six times.
- Magnussen left mid-season for CART. Montoya (round 7) and Zonta (round 8) stood in.
- Tarquini won at Silverstone but had poor luck otherwise.
- Wurz was in the Joest Opel line-up (Joest #25).
- Media and TV exposure was poor. ZDF dropped the series just before the season and VOX replaced it. Attendance fell because of high ticket prices and a closed paddock.
