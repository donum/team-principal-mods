# DTM 1993 – research dossier

10th DTM season, first under FIA Class 1 rules. 10 championship events × 2 heats = 20 points-scoring races (4 Apr Zolder – 19 Sep Hockenheim), plus the non-championship Donington Gold Cup (18 Jul, 2 heats). Champion: **Nicola Larini** (Alfa Romeo 155 V6 TI, Alfa Corse), 261 pts. Manufacturers: Alfa Romeo 332, Mercedes-Benz 320. Teams: Alfa Corse 382.

## Regulations
- **Class 1** (main class): max 6 cylinders, 2.5 litres, 4 valves/cylinder. The engine must come from a production engine by the same manufacturer, but not necessarily from the raced model (en.wikipedia "Class 1 Touring Cars"). The body had to come from a road model built in at least 25,000 units (de.wikipedia).
- Engine, chassis and aero could be heavily modified. Aero was free below the wheel centre/hub line; rear wing size and position were fixed. Aero packages could change race to race (e.g. different wings for AVUS and the Nordschleife) and even overnight (Alfa added side-skirt brake ducts at the Norisring) without homologation requests (de.wikipedia).
- Electronics and drivetrain: 4WD, traction control, ABS and electronically controlled differentials were permitted (en.wikipedia). In 1993 Mercedes and all BMW privateers ran ABS; Alfa only got ABS on its 1994 car. Mercedes brought traction control and active suspension from F1 (de.wikipedia). TCR says Mercedes first used traction control at Donington. Active Body Control was fitted first to Schneider's car mid-season per de.wikipedia; TCR has Asch winning Diepholz heat 1 with active suspension.
- Fuel consumption was capped at 55 l/100 km (de.wikipedia, citing Voigt, *Tourenwagen-Story '93*).
- Old Group A cars (1992 Mercedes 190E Evo II, BMW M3 Sport Evo, Ford Mustang, Opel Omega 3.8) were still admitted and classified as Class 1 (TCR race tables). Kissling's Omega was allowed a 3.8 engine to stay competitive (TCR).
- Background: BMW and then Audi withdrew in protest at an ONS handicap clause. The clause was revoked, but neither returned. Opel postponed its entry. Norbert Haug persuaded the Mercedes board to stay (de.wikipedia, citing Voigt).
- **Class 2** ("2000 Cup"): 2.0-litre Class 2 cars, numbers from 51 (en.wikipedia). The cup was a flop: 5 entries at Zolder, of which 2 appeared and none finished, and no Class 2 car qualified at the Norisring (TCR). No Class 2 standings were found.
- **Points** (each heat, top 10): 20-15-12-10-8-6-4-3-2-1. No bonus points (de/en.wikipedia).
- **Weights**: TCR car data gives 1,040 kg for the Alfa 155 V6 TI (4WD) and 1,000 kg for the Mercedes 190E Class 1. The regulation minimum-weight table was not found. TCR notes that at the Nordschleife the works Mercedes drivers, now all in 1993-spec cars, had "no weight penalty at all", which implies some weight-penalty scheme. Not verified.

## Weekend format
- Free practice, timed qualifying, then a **Top-10 shoot-out** (TCR: Zolder, Hockenheim). TCR calls it "top-15 qualifying" at the Norisring and the Hockenheim finale.
- Two heats of about 100 km each (no qualification race). The heat 2 grid followed the heat 1 finishing order, so heat 1 retirements started at the back (TCR Hockenheim narrative: Larini and Ludwig started heat 2 from the back).
- **No mandatory pit stop.** Stops were only for tyres or damage (e.g. the change to slicks in AVUS heat 1).

Mercedes began the year with converted "Klasse-1B" interim cars and a mix of Evo IIs. Full Class 1 190Es arrived during the season (de.wikipedia "AMG-Mercedes 190E Klasse 1"). The C-Class was not ready.

## Teams / people (documented)
- Alfa Corse (works, 4 cars incl. Schübel Engineering): boss Giorgio Pianta, engine man Pino D'Agostino (it.wikipedia). The quoted engineer Giorgio Pantana is cited in de.wikipedia.
- Mercedes-Benz motorsport chief: Norbert Haug. Works cars came from AMG (TCR entrant "AMG Motorenbau GmbH"), split into "AMG-Mercedes Berlin 2000" and "AMG-Mercedes Sonax", plus Diebels Zakspeed.
- Opel sport chief: Walter Treser. Calibras were run by Joest (de.wikipedia "Opel Calibra V6 4×4").
- Linder: Luggi Linder (TCR).

## Storylines
- Larini dominated: 10 championship heat wins plus Donington heat 2, a Nordschleife double on his first visit, and the title clinched at AVUS heat 1.
- Nannini's bad luck (punctures, botched AVUS stop) ended with a tearful double at the Hockenheim finale.
- The Mercedes 1993 car was crash-developed. Ludwig won Eifel heat 2 in the old Evo II.
- Opel Calibra debut at the finale: Reuter qualified 5th, Rosberg finished 7th in heat 1, and both cars retired in heat 2 with electronics problems.
- The Class 2 cup failed. The field was otherwise filled with private 1992 BMW M3s, Mustangs and Omegas.

- Göbel's first name: de.wikipedia says "Alexander", TCR says "Axel".

- TCR gives the Švarc Omega's displacement as "3780?".
