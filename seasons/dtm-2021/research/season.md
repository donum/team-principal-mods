# 2021 DTM: research dossier

## Overview
- 35th DTM season and the first under GT3 rules.
- Calendar: 8 weekends × 2 races = 16 races, 19 June (Monza) to 10 October (Norisring).
- Organiser: ITR (Gerhard Berger). This was ITR's second-to-last season.
- Champion: **Maximilian Götz** (Mercedes-AMG Team HRT), 230 points, ahead of Liam Lawson (227, Ferrari) and Kelvin van der Linde (208, Audi).
- Teams' title: Red Bull AlphaTauri AF Corse (Ferrari).
- Manufacturers' title: Mercedes-AMG.
- Junior classification: won by Lawson.
- The title was decided in the last race at the Norisring. Lawson and K. van der Linde collided in the first corner. In the closing laps, Mercedes-AMG team mates let Götz past. As a result, team orders were banned again from 2022 (en/de.wiki).

## Regulations
- **Cars:** FIA GT3 (ISG Annex J Art. 257A), run under the DTM's own Balance of Performance. Class 1 (2.0 L turbo I4) was dropped after only two seasons. ABS and traction control were allowed; there were no driver changes (motorsport.com, 2020).
- **Starts:** rolling, two-abreast "DTM formation start". Standing starts were dropped for the first time since 2001.
- **Tyres:** Michelin, a single supplier. Hankook ended its contract (due to run to 2023) early. Tyre allocation per weekend was not verified.
- **BoP:** calculated by AVL track by track. From the 4th event onwards, BoP could no longer be changed during a race weekend (de.wiki 2022 page, looking back).
- **Success ballast (Erfolgsballast)**, carried in the next race only:
  - 1st place: +25 kg
  - 2nd place: +18 kg
  - 3rd place: +15 kg
  - Source: de.wiki 2022, comparing with 2021 values.
- **Race:** 55 min + 1 lap, single driver, both Saturday and Sunday.
  - One mandatory pit stop with all four wheels changed.
  - According to de.wiki 2022, the stop was allowed "from lap 6" in 2021. A weak source says there was no time window. Unverified.
  - A tyre change under the safety car did not count as the mandatory stop.
  - There was no rear-wheels-first rule yet (introduced in 2022).
- **Qualifying:** one 20-minute session before each race. Qualifying points: 3-2-1 (unchanged format through 2026).
- **Race points:** top 10 scored 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1. No fastest-lap point.
- **Manufacturers' points:** at most the 3 best cars of a make per race, including qualifying (de.wiki).
- **Push-to-pass / DRS:** none documented. Class 1 DRS and push-to-pass ended with that formula.
- **Guest drivers** ("G") were not eligible for points.

## Teams and people
- **Audi:** factory support ended after 2020. Abt and Rosberg continued as private Audi teams.
- **Abt:**
  - Drivers: K. van der Linde, Rockenfeller and Sophia Flörsch.
  - Flörsch was the first woman in the DTM since 2012.
  - Abt also ran a guest car for Lucas di Grassi.
- **New teams:**
  - AF Corse: Red Bull-backed Ferraris for Albon and Lawson.
  - HRT: founded by Hubert Haupt.
  - Winward: US team, linked to HTP.
  - GruppeM: Hong Kong.
  - GetSpeed: Arjun Maini, the first Indian DTM driver.
  - Walkenhorst and ROWE: BMW M6.
  - T3: Lamborghini.
  - JP Motorsport: McLaren, guest entries only.
  - Mücke returned to the DTM.
- **Withdrawals:** Jenson Team Rocket RJN (McLaren) was announced but withdrew in March 2021.
- **Gary Paffett:** entered by Mücke but never raced. Maximilian Buhk drove the car.
- **Team principals:**
  - Not systematically documented in the sources used.
  - From general knowledge, not checked: Abt – Thomas Biermaier; HRT – Ulrich Fritz (CEO); Rosberg – Kimmo Liimatainen.

## Storylines
- René Rast (2020 champion) left for Formula E. Robert Kubica also left.
- Lawson: Red Bull junior who combined the DTM with F2. He led into the finale.
- Albon: Red Bull reserve driver. He missed only the finale, where Nick Cassidy replaced him.
- First wins for:
  - Ferrari (Lawson, Monza R1)
  - Lamborghini: none in 2021. Bortolotti, as a guest, was 2nd at Assen.
  - BMW M6 (Wittmann, Zolder R2)
