# 2011 Formula One World Championship: research dossier

## Overview
- 62nd F1 World Championship. 19 rounds from 27 March (Melbourne) to 27 November (Interlagos).
- The Bahrain Grand Prix was first postponed and then cancelled. New race: the Indian Grand Prix (Buddh International Circuit). Turkey was held for the last time in this era. The German Grand Prix was at the Nürburgring.
- 12 teams, 24 cars, 28 race drivers.
- First year of Pirelli as single tyre supplier (after Bridgestone), DRS and the return of KERS.
- Champion: **Sebastian Vettel** (Red Bull Racing, Red Bull RB7), 392 pts, 11 wins, ahead of Jenson Button (270). He clinched the title in Japan (round 15) with four races left.
- Constructors: Red Bull Racing 650, McLaren 497, Ferrari 375, Mercedes 165, Lotus Renault GP 73.
- Red Bull started 18 of 19 races from grid 1 (Vettel 15, Webber 3). Only Hamilton in Korea broke the run.
- Five different winners: Vettel 11, Button 3, Hamilton 3, Alonso 1, Webber 1.

## Regulations
### Points
- 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 for the top 10. Classified at 90% of the winner's laps.

### Qualifying
- Knockout Q1 (20 min, slowest 7 out), Q2 (15 min, slowest 7 out), Q3 (10 min, top 10).
- 107% rule reintroduced for 2011. Both HRT cars missed it in Australia and did not start.
- The top 10 start on the tyres of their fastest Q3 lap.

### Tyres (Pirelli, first season)
- Dry compounds: hard (silver), medium (white), soft (yellow), supersoft (red). Intermediate and wet for rain.
- Two dry compounds per race, harder "prime" and softer "option". Both must be used in a dry race.
- Pirelli was asked for tyres that wear quickly. At the final Barcelona test teams expected up to three pit stops per race (en.wiki season article).
- Pirelli brought a new "extra hard" tyre (en.wiki: about two seconds per lap slower) to Spain; en.wiki also mentions it for Turkey.
- Belgium: blistering in qualifying (Red Bull ran camber outside Pirelli's recommendation). From Monza Pirelli's camber limits were mandatory.

### DRS
- New for 2011. In the race only in the marked zone, only within one second of the car ahead at the detection point, not on the first two laps. Unlimited in practice and qualifying, not allowed in wet conditions. It shuts when the driver touches the brakes.
- One zone at most races. A second zone was tried from Canada.

### KERS
- Returned in 2011 after a year off. 60 kW, 400 kJ per lap (about 6.67 s per lap).

### Other rules
- No refuelling. Minimum weight 640 kg with driver (raised from 620 kg in 2010 for the heavier Pirelli tyres and KERS; not stated in the fetched articles, general knowledge).
- 2.4 V8, 18,000 rpm, engines frozen, 8 engines per driver per season. Gearbox for 5 events; one free "joker" gearbox change per season was still allowed (dropped for 2012, en.wiki 2012 article).
- Double diffusers banned, diffuser height cut from 175 mm to 125 mm. McLaren's driver-operated F-duct banned.
- Off-throttle blown diffusers: the FIA restricted them at the British Grand Prix only. After Silverstone the teams agreed to go back to the earlier rules for the rest of 2011. A full ban followed in 2012.
- In-season testing banned.

## Driver changes in the season
- Robert Kubica was injured in a rally crash before the season. Nick Heidfeld took his seat at Lotus Renault GP for rounds 1-11. Bruno Senna replaced Heidfeld from Belgium (round 12).
- Sergio Pérez crashed heavily in Monaco qualifying and did not start that race. Pedro de la Rosa drove his Sauber in Canada (round 7).
- Karun Chandhok drove Jarno Trulli's Team Lotus at the Nürburgring (round 10).
- Daniel Ricciardo, a Toro Rosso reserve, replaced Narain Karthikeyan at HRT from Silverstone (round 9). Karthikeyan raced again at his home race in India in place of Liuzzi.

## Story of the season
- **Red Bull dominance.** The RB7 used the exhaust gases to blow the diffuser, also when the driver was off the throttle (engine maps kept the gas flowing). Vettel started from pole in 15 races and won 11. He won 5 of the first 6 races and led the championship from round 1 to the end.
- **Blown-diffuser row.** The FIA restricted off-throttle blowing at Silverstone. Red Bull did not win there (Alonso won, Ferrari's only win of 2011). After the race the old rules came back for the rest of the year.
- **McLaren second.** Hamilton won China, Germany and Abu Dhabi. Button won Canada, Hungary and Japan. Button's Canada win came after a four-hour race with a long red flag, and he passed Vettel on the last lap.
- **Ferrari.** Consistent podiums but only one win. Ferrari had the most trouble with the new extra-hard tyre in Spain (Alonso finished a lap down).
- **Renault faded.** Petrov and Heidfeld scored podiums in Australia and Malaysia (30 points in two races), then Renault scored 43 points in the next 17 races.
- **Force India rose.** 20 points in rounds 1-10, 49 in rounds 11-19, beating Sauber and Toro Rosso to 6th.
- **Williams** scored 5 points, 9th of 12.
